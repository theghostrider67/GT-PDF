# Evidence, citations, terminology, and IEEE style

## Citation integrity

Never invent authors, titles, papers, DOIs, venues, years, URLs, or citation numbers. If a needed source is unavailable, write `[CITATION NEEDED]`. When references are supplied, restrict source-dependent claims to what those references support. Preserve existing citations and their placement unless moving one is necessary for clarity and does not change its scope.

## Technical terminology

Keep established terms consistent. In machine-learning and computer-science writing, do not casually replace terms such as *knowledge graph*, *retrieval-augmented generation*, *reinforcement learning*, *embedding*, *inference*, *fine-tuning*, *attention*, *graph compression*, *reward function*, *retrieval*, *precision*, *recall*, and *F1-score* merely for lexical variety.

## IEEE conventions

When IEEE style is requested:

- Use concise, formal technical prose and avoid conversational language.
- Avoid unnecessary headings and excessive bullet lists within manuscript sections.
- Preserve numeric citations such as `[1]`, `[2]`, and `[3]`; do not renumber them unless instructed.
- Keep mathematical symbols, capitalization, abbreviations, and units consistent.
- Preserve references such as `Fig. 1` and `Table I`.
- Use compact paragraphs, but do not sacrifice logical development or create a monotonous rhythm.

Publisher style never overrides factual accuracy, evidence discipline, or the author's explicit constraints.
