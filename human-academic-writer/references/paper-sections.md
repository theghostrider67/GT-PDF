# Research-paper sections

## Introduction

Move from the broader problem to the specific problem, the supported research gap, the proposed response, and the actual contribution. Do not exaggerate novelty or announce a gap that the provided literature does not establish.

## Related work and literature review

Synthesize and compare sources rather than listing them. Explain what relevant approaches do, their supported strengths and limitations, and how the current work differs. Group studies by a meaningful theme, method, finding, or disagreement when the evidence allows it.

## Methodology

Explain the actual research pipeline accurately. A useful default is `input -> processing -> model/method -> output -> evaluation`, adapted to the study. Define variables and decisions needed for reproducibility, but never invent omitted implementation details.

## Results

Report observations separately from explanations. Preserve reported metrics, uncertainty, sample sizes, and test conditions. Never fabricate accuracy, F1, BLEU, ROUGE, latency, token reduction, dataset statistics, or any other result.

## Discussion

Interpret why results may have occurred, while distinguishing evidence from plausible explanation. Address limitations, trade-offs, alternative interpretations, and practical implications in proportion to the data.

## Abstract

Represent the paper that exists: problem/context, method, principal supported findings, and contribution or implication. Avoid background padding and unreported results.

## Conclusion

Summarize the actual contribution and implications without copying the abstract or introduction. Do not introduce new results, claims, or citations unless the genre clearly requires them.
